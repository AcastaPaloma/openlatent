# Literature scout: robotics, embodied AI and driving

Scout round 1, 2026-11-04. Scope: where a learned mask or latent `z = E(x)` keeps a task signal and removes or isolates a nuisance (embodiment, background, distractor, identity), and the result can be checked deterministically.

How I read sources: every paper below was opened with WebFetch. "H" = full HTML read; "A" = arXiv abstract page only (so benchmark names and numbers are only what the abstract page showed). Numbers marked "A" are the authors' own claims. Where a search snippet said something the opened page did not support, I say so. Dataset licences I could not open are marked "unverified". I did not run anything.

- Data: DROID [2403.12945](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12945); RoboCasa365 [2603.04356](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.04356); LIBERO README (H).

Pattern across the papers: the mask works when nuisance and task occupy different pixels (arm vs object; background vs agent). It fails or is unneeded when they overlap: the wrist-camera gripper is both the task signal and the embodiment (Cloak has to special-case it), distractors whose motion tracks the action (paper 16's own stated limit; paper 11 reports UniVLA does not help), camera viewpoint (paper 17). This is the robotics version of our Charades finding.

### C2. Distractor- and embodiment-free latent actions from video (27)
- Case: the latent action `z = E(frame_t, frame_t+1)` must carry only agent-controlled motion; today it absorbs backgrounds, camera shake and other movers, and every fix so far is hand-wired (SAM masks in 14, slots in 12, a prompted VLM target in 13). Our contribution would be a learned mask/latent trained with retrained nuisance probes, scored both ways: how well true actions are linearly decodable from z (task kept) and how well distractor/background/embodiment state is decodable by a freshly trained probe (nuisance removed), including on distractor types unseen in training.
- Data: MetaWorld scripted experts (5k trajectories per task in paper 13) and Distracting Control Suite / Distracting Meta-World (papers 12, 14) are generated, so size is ours to choose; real distractors in paper 13 are DAVIS clips (licence unverified). Real video for a later stage: DROID (76k trajectories, 350 h, 564 scenes, abstract says the full dataset is open, paper listing CC BY 4.0; dataset-specific licence not confirmed), OXE (per-dataset licences, unverified), Ego4D (gated; licence terms not confirmed, see C4).
- Benchmark: Distracting Meta-World / DCS plus the MetaWorld MT10 twin-render protocol of paper 13. Twin renders (same state, with and without distractor) give an exact ground truth for what the distractor contributed, so leakage can be computed offline and exactly; linear probe of true actions is a deterministic number given fixed seeds and frozen data. This fits OpenLatent's "score z deterministically" most directly of all candidates.
- Biggest risk: crowded (four papers in 12 months, one an AAAI-26 oral) and toy-scale; "better linear probe" may not translate to policy return, and action-correlated distractors are exactly the case where a mask cannot separate the signals.

### C1. Embodiment-blind observations for cross-robot transfer (25)
- Case: embodiment is the identity of robotics. A learned arm/gripper mask or an embodiment-adversarial latent should make the policy input look the same across robots, replacing the URDF + calibrated-camera masks of Shadow (1) and Cloak (3) and the open-vocabulary-model masks of ARRO (2). Verification mirrors our harness: retrain an embodiment classifier on z (robot ID, from OXE or from sim robots) next to an action probe and a closed-loop success number.
- Data: DROID (above); OXE is skewed (top 4 robot types >85% of real data, paper 6); OXE-AugE adds 9 rendered embodiments, licence unstated; robomimic/MimicGen sim demos for Shadow-style tasks (RoboCasa assets/datasets are CC BY 4.0, code MIT per its README).
- Benchmark: build "Shadow-sim": Panda source to Sawyer/IIWA/UR5e in MuJoCo, 6 tasks, 100 rollouts, as in paper 1 (not released as a benchmark that I could confirm); plus an offline probe benchmark on OXE/DROID subsets. Closed-loop rollouts are deterministic only up to GPU nondeterminism (I found no determinism claim in 1, 2 or 17), so report seeds and intervals.
- Biggest risk: the geometric mask is already at ceiling in sim (0.97 vs 0.98), so a learned method has no headroom unless the claim is "no URDF, no calibration, unseen embodiment". Shadow's own ablation also shows that masking the source robot alone does nothing; what transfers is covering the unseen target robot's out-of-distribution pixels, which a learned mask must do without ever seeing the target robot.

### C4. Privacy-preserving egocentric / home-robot perception (24)
- Case: the same game as the current programme (task kept, identity or household secrets removed) in a setting where a real need exists: egocentric data sharing and cloud offload from home robots. EgoPrivacy (19) supplies the attack side with no defence track, AAM (AAM, ICLR 2026) works on non-egocentric video, and TFPD (20) shows that equal task success hides a 0.53-0.97 spread in linkability, which is our "where the secret lives" result in a robot setting.
- Data: Ego4D and EPIC-KITCHENS are gated, terms unverified (a commercial blog says EPIC is CC BY-NC and Ego4D has partner-specific terms; I did not open the official pages); AI2-THOR / ProcTHOR scenes are free to generate; EgoBlur is Apache 2.0 per Meta's docs (search result, not opened; a baseline, not a learned method).
- Benchmark: TFPD protocol on 120 AI2-THOR scenes (scene-disjoint splits, navigation success, residual-leakage scores) is simulator-based and checkable; its attacker protocol is "frozen attacker selection" plus held-out attacks, so we would add retrained attackers. EgoPrivacy covers the real-video attack side and still needs a defence track.
- Biggest risk: it is the user's stated worry. In a home the identity is mostly the room (our Charades result: removing people left 91% identity), so the likely outcome is the same negative result, and it does not help the robot's own task, only data sharing.

### C3. Task-relevant / object-centric masks for policy visual robustness (23)
- Case: a learned mask or slot latent should keep gripper and target and drop texture, lighting, distractors. Evidence is mixed: slots win on texture and lighting, Theia wins on distractors in MetaWorld (paper 8), and LIBERO-Plus (17) says the real failures are camera viewpoint and initial state, not distractors or lighting.
- Data: LIBERO code MIT and datasets CC BY 4.0 (per README, 130 tasks, fixed initial states via get_task_init_states); RoboCasa365 CC BY 4.0 (365 tasks, 2,500 kitchens, 600+ h human demos); COLOSSEUM and LIBERO-Plus code open, licences not stated in the papers (LIBERO-Plus repo had no explicit licence per a search summary).
- Benchmark: COLOSSEUM (20 tasks, 14 axes), LIBERO-Plus (10,030 tasks), Distracting MetaWorld. Closed-loop, so noisy: paper 8 used 3 seeds x 50 rollouts and its real-world set only 10 rollouts; policy training per method per suite makes it the most expensive of the four.
- Biggest risk: the bottleneck on the best benchmarks is not what a mask fixes (camera pose), and it is crowded (8, 9, 2, 14, CGVD).

### C6. Driving: appearance/PII-free planner latents (18), do not pursue first
- Case: planners degrade under appearance shift (navdream), and frozen DINOv3 features are the current zero-shot fix; PII blurring is a solved detection problem for practical purposes (BlurScene reported 96% face and 98% plate recall; EgoBlur 63%/71% at the same false-positive budget, from a search result, not opened).
- Data/benchmark: NAVSIM (code Apache 2.0; nuPlan/OpenScene keep their own licences, sizes not stated on the page; pseudo-simulation computed open-loop) and Bench2Drive (CARLA closed-loop). navdream is not yet released.
- Biggest risk: huge data, CARLA closed-loop cost and nondeterminism, no clean privacy-vs-task trade-off to measure. BridgeSim says the open/closed-loop gap is mostly objective mismatch, which no mask touches.

### C5. Segmentation-space sim-to-real world models (16), would NOT pursue
- Case: predict masks, render pixels (Mask2Real-WM, RAP); masks as a domain-invariant interface. Interesting idea, but the mask here is given, not learned or removed, and the only evaluation I found is human-rated controllability on one 23-DoF hand with 2.5 h of real data, so there is no deterministic benchmark to fix and we would be building both the benchmark and the method on hardware we do not have.

## 3. Recommendation

Pursue C2 first (best fit with `z = E(x)` scored by a deterministic probe; cheap; twin-render ground truth), then C1 as the robotics-flavoured closed-loop companion (embodiment as the "identity"), with C4 as the continuity candidate that reuses the existing harness. Treat C3 as a downstream check on C1/C2 latents rather than a field of its own. Do not pursue C5; park C6.

What I could not verify: determinism of any closed-loop benchmark (no paper claims bitwise determinism); licences for Meta-World, Distracting Control Suite, DAVIS, ManiSkill, CALVIN, Ego4D, OXE sub-datasets, EgoPrivacy data (CALVIN and ManiSkill I did not open at all); full text of papers marked A.
