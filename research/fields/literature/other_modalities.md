# Scout report: non-visual and cross-modal signals as candidate fields

Scout date 2026-11-04. Cluster: speech/audio, radar/WiFi/mmWave, smart-home, keystroke/touch, gaze/XR motion, text, tabular/EHR, genomics, LiDAR, remote sensing, time-series/physiological foundation models.
Question (CHARTER): where does a learned mask or latent `z = E(x)` that keeps a task signal and removes/isolates a nuisance or sensitive property have a live need, a field that is not saturated, and a deterministic benchmark?

How to read this file. "Read" column: **F** = full text or long HTML extract opened, **A** = abstract page only (numbers are the authors' abstract claims; I did not see tables). Everything cited below was opened through WebFetch; items I only saw in search snippets are listed separately at the end and are not relied on. WebFetch summarises with a small model, so numbers are as returned; where the source contradicted itself I say so. All scores are my judgement.

### C. Single-cell batch-invariant latents (cell type = task, batch/donor/assay = nuisance)
- **Case.** The integrated embedding is the deliverable, and 2025 papers (#16, #17) show foundation-model latents still carry batch while the community's batch scores are heuristics (kBET, iLISI, ASW) plus linear probes. Our transferable finding, that privacy numbers are inflated until a strong attacker is retrained on the obfuscated latent, has not been applied here: a nonlinear batch classifier retrained on each integrated latent, with bootstrap over donors, would be a stricter evaluator on a benchmark where scIB code and public atlases already fix everything else.
- **Data/benchmark.** CELLxGENE collections (HLCA core `6f6d381a-...`, 585k cells), BMMC GSE194122, scIB reproducibility repo; one A100 sufficed in #17. Licences are per dataset; I did not verify them (hence 4). scIB metrics are existing deterministic code; the gap is the evaluator, not the metric suite.
- **Biggest risk.** Batch is often confounded with biology (batch = tissue, donor, species in #16's scenarios), so "nuisance removed" has no clean ground truth, and a stronger-attacker paper may read as metric nit-picking to a mature, crowded community (scIB since 2022, dozens of methods, scIB-E 2025). No privacy framing; this is a nuisance-isolation case.

### A. Gaze / eye-tracking privacy (task = usable gaze, nuisance = biometric identity)
- **Case.** Gaze streams are needed raw by foveated rendering and interaction, yet identify people; only two learned-obfuscation papers exist (#7, #8) and the one with a retrained attacker (#7) uses a single attacker and task and perturbs only spatial coordinates, while identity also lives in temporal dynamics. GazeBase (322 subjects, 1000 Hz, **CC BY 4.0 verified** from the dataset paper) and GazeBaseVR (407 participants, 5,020 recordings) make a cheap, fast sandbox; hours per run.
- **Benchmark we would build.** Fixed subject-disjoint splits, enrol/verify across sessions as in #7, a worst-case attacker suite (EKYT-style CNN, a transformer, a hand-feature classifier, all retrained on the privatised train set), utility = task of record (prediction error, fixation/saccade event F1, target selection from #8), chance and trivial-baseline normalisation. EKYT code release not confirmed in my search; GazeBaseVR licence not verified.
- **Biggest risk.** #8 suggests the winning "privacy" is just identity-independent noise, so a learned method may add little, and the utility task is weak and application-dependent; the whole field is small and may offer no strong reference numbers to beat.

### B. XR body-motion anonymisation (head+hands, BOXRR-23)
- **Case.** Closest analogue of the existing KTH work (identity from motion style) with a live threat (re-identification stays near-perfect under simple degradation, #12; 4.7 M recordings from 105,852 users exist, #11) and a learned defence (#10) whose headline adaptive-attack result sits at exactly chance for one attacker family, which is the kind of number the worst-case-evaluator rule exists to audit. A one-chunk subset (about 45 GB, 1,000 users) is enough, and the anonymiser has 65k parameters.
- **Benchmark we would build.** DMM's oblivious+adaptive protocol, frozen on a fixed 1,000-user subset, with additional attacker architectures not used in the anonymiser loss, cross-session and cross-app tests (Tilt Brush), and an utility measure beyond Beat Saber replay fidelity.
- **Biggest risk.** Licence is CC BY-NC-SA with a DUA (fine for research and HF weights under NC, restrictive otherwise); utility is specific to one game; and it overlaps strongly with the video/skeleton programme (low diversification). Adjacent skeleton work (#14) may already cover our canonical-skeleton idea; I could not see its numbers.

### E. Speech anonymisation (VoicePrivacy Challenge 2026)
- **Case.** Best benchmark in this cluster: fixed trial lists, fixed ASR/SER models, a semi-informed attacker retrained on anonymised data, released scripts, and a live fourth edition with a stronger attacker (cross-gender trials, attacker pre-trained on voice-converted speech, per-language fine-tuning). The "where the secret lives" lens fits: after timbre is gone, identity survives in duration/prosody (#5) and in the words (#4).
- **Why scored low on novelty.** 13 teams / 36 systems in 2024, every strong entry already learned, the retrained-attacker lesson already the protocol (#1, #2), and years of tuning by specialised groups. Compute is also not trivial: the codec-LM baseline B4 is listed at about 72 h, and each submission needs an attacker retrain.
- **Biggest risk.** Entering a crowded, moving target (trial lists were fixed on 2026-09-01; plan version numbers disagree across sources: v0.0 PDF vs v1.2 on GitHub) for a result that reviewers will compare with dedicated systems; IEMOCAP access takes 7-9 days. Variant: clinical-speech anonymisation that keeps a disease marker (#6) is more novel but its corpus is not shown to be open, so I do not score it separately.

### D. Subject-identity removal in EEG/physiological foundation models
- **Case.** Two June-2026 audits (#18, #19) show frozen EEG-FM embeddings are dominated by subject identity and leak spectral attributes, and that DP-SGD does nothing; the only fix tried is closed-form linear LEACE, with learned/adversarial alternatives explicitly left as future work. Public data exist (EEGMAT on PhysioNet, ADFTD on OpenNeuro) and the evaluation is frozen features, so runs take minutes.
- **Biggest risk.** Cohorts of 17-65 subjects make "bootstrap over independent units" and a sealed test split nearly meaningless (confidence intervals will be wide), identity and biomarker are physically entangled (aperiodic 1/f carries both in LaBraM/CBraMod), and LEACE already drives linear subject decoding to chance, so the room for a learned method is the nonlinear residual (1.6 % in one cell). A concurrent LEACE study (Tang et al. 2026, cited inside #18) shows the area is moving; FM weight licences were not checked.

### F. Wearable-IMU HAR identity removal (MotionSense, PAMAP2)
- **Case.** Same shape as our work, tiny and cheap, and the datasets are small public ones (24 subjects in MotionSense per the secondary summary I saw; not verified by me). It could serve as a one-evening sanity check of the harness.
- **Biggest risk.** Saturated since the 2019-20 anonymising autoencoder, #15 does not state its attacker, cohorts are too small for the evaluation rules, and the finding would only repeat Finding 1. Score reflects novelty 1.

## 3. Recommendation and what I would not pursue

**Order for a contrarian round to try to beat:** C (single-cell batch-invariant latents), then A (gaze), then B (XR body motion). A and B share infrastructure (1-D streams, same attacker/evaluator code), so pursuing both is cheap; C is the most different from the existing programme and tests the retrained-attacker thesis in a field that has not applied it.

**Would NOT pursue:**
- **E, speech, as a research field.** Keep VoicePrivacy as the protocol template (and possibly as a sanity yardstick for OpenLatent's scoring of fixed models), not as a place to compete.
- **D, EEG-FM,** unless a cohort with hundreds of subjects with an open licence is found: the sealed-split and bootstrap rules cannot be satisfied at n = 17-65.
- **F, wearable IMU.** Old, saturated, tiny.
- **Text (author/PII masking).** The standard attacker is an LLM (ICLR/ACL 2025 anonymiser-vs-adversary setups appeared only in search results), which is versioned and often API-based, so it fails the deterministic-evaluation rule unless frozen to an open model, and the 2025-26 literature is dense. I did not open the text papers.
- **Tabular/EHR fair representations.** A decade-old saturated literature; my one search found no 2025 benchmark that retrains the adversary, and clinical data are credentialed.
- **LiDAR, remote sensing.** LiDAR: re-id from shape works (#20) but I found no learned-removal papers, no public detection-vs-identity benchmark; remote sensing: location is the signal in the recent embedding literature (SatCLIP, WildSAT appeared in search), and I found no sensitive-property demand.
- **WiFi CSI / mmWave radar.** Defences found target blinding the sensor, not keep-activity/remove-identity; benchmark leakage issues are known in CSI HAR; hardware-bound data.
- **Smart-home, keystroke/touch.** Nothing relevant found beyond dataset pages.
- **Audio deepfake detection.** Speaker-invariance via gradient reversal exists (arXiv [2602.20805](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.20805), abstract only: -17 % average EER), but that is robustness of a detector, not mask production; ASVspoof 5 licence unclear (CC BY-NC-SA icon on [2502.08857](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08857), dataset terms unchecked).

## 4. Cross-field pattern (links to the privacy-obfuscation findings)
- Retrained attackers are already standard in speech (#1), appear once in gaze (#7) and in XR (#10 "adaptive"), and are mostly absent in single-cell (linear probes only, #16) and EEG (#18 uses a linear probe + MLP residual). That absence is the gap in C and D.
- "Where the secret lives": speech identity survives in prosody/duration/lexical content (#4, #5); gaze identity in temporal dynamics (#7 limits); body identity in motion style (#10, #12); single-cell batch is confounded with donor/tissue (#16). In each case a single fixed-transform baseline would not hide it, which matches our Finding 4.
- A sceptic caveat common to A, B, D: reported near-chance adaptive-attack numbers (#10 at 0.1 %, #7 at 10.5 %) come from one attacker family or one task, so any of these fields needs the worst-case suite before a number is believed.
