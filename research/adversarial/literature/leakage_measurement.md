# Literature scout: leakage measurement and guarantees (2024-2026 first)

Scout for the adversary-suite programme (programme 2). Written 2026-11-04. Cluster: how to measure leakage of a mask or latent without depending on one chosen attacker;
attacker-independent bounds; privacy auditing; calibration of "enough attackers"; attacker scaling; reporting a worst case over attackers with an honest interval.

Read depth tags used below. [A] = the arXiv/ePrint abstract page was opened (abstract and metadata only, no theorems or tables). [H] = the HTML body was opened (first ~100k characters).
[S] = seen only in a search snippet, NOT opened; listed at the end and not used for any claim. All arXiv IDs below were opened unless tagged [S]. Many 2026 items are unreviewed preprints and
every number I quote is the authors' own claim from the abstract unless I say "my arithmetic". I did not run code.

## 1. The one idea that organises this cluster

Three different kinds of statement get called "leakage guarantee", and mixing them is the main source of overclaiming:

1. Lower bound on leakage, valid for any attacker you actually ran (retrained attacker accuracy; calibrated log-loss = V-information; one-run audit lower bounds on epsilon). Programme 1 lives here.
2. Suite-conditional upper bound: "no attacker in this pre-declared finite suite exceeds x on unseen units, with confidence 1-alpha" (finite-sample, union bound). Cheap and deterministic, but silent about attackers outside the suite.
3. Model-based upper bound valid against all attackers (DP / capacity / Fano / maximal leakage). Needs structure in the release (calibrated noise, quantisation, bounded sensitivity). A learned adapter with no injected randomness does not have one.

The Bayes-error and MI-estimator literature does not escape this: a consistent estimator is an asymptotic statement, not a finite-sample certificate for the dangerous direction ("attackers cannot do better than x").
McAllester and Stratos (1811.04251 [A]) prove distribution-free high-confidence MI lower bounds cannot exceed O(ln N), so a large leak cannot even be certified from samples without assumptions.

## 3. What the literature says, by question

- V-usable / predictive V-information. For a discrete secret like identity, the cross-entropy of a retrained, calibrated attacker gives I_V = H(ID) - CE, a lower bound on Shannon MI (standard identity, not from a 2024+ paper). I found no 2024-2026 paper that applies V-information as a privacy-suite statistic; the closest are Gupta et al. (AutoML Bayes approximation) and Makonnen et al. (calibrated classifiers). Gap, but a thin one.
- Bayes-error estimators. Best nonparametric choice is kNN (Wheat et al.), but sample needs (>=1,000 per class, binary) are far above HAR data. A kNN Bayes-error estimate is an attacker that is already in the suite. Their value is as a convergence diagnostic validated with FeeBee-style injected noise, not as a number to report.
- MI estimators (MINE, InfoNCE, CLUB). Lower-bound family underestimates, CLUB-type overshoots, InfoNCE is capped near ln(batch) (from a 2025 high-dim study snippet, abstract page did not confirm), nothing is a certificate. For identity use the classifier cross-entropy; reserve MI estimators for continuous attributes.
- Attacker-independent bounds. Exist only with structure: DP/f-DP audits, Fano, maximal leakage, PAC-privacy simulation. For a deterministic learned encoder, DP epsilon is infinite for distinct inputs; "identity-level" notions (#7) sidestep this by treating the image distribution of an identity as the randomness.
- Auditing 2024-2026. Rapid progress on one-run audits (#9-#10, Agrawal 2026) and post-hoc audits (#11), all for membership under DP-style randomisation. Directly reusable idea: split dev/test, fix the rule, one-sided Clopper-Pearson, simultaneous correction (#7). Not directly reusable: canary machinery (needs controlled training).
- Certified leakage for learned encoders. I found nothing certified for HAR or CT encoders. Search for provable/certified anonymisation bounds for face, voice, gait returned only empirical papers. Brain-MRI foundation-embedding leakage (2610.20494) is empirical with a single attacker.
- "How many attackers are enough". No paper found that gives an extreme-value or sequential stopping rule for a suite. Closest: Adversarial Frontiers (monotone in budget, no bound), risk-compute curves, BoN power-law forecasting, rare-behaviour forecasting. The EVT route (Weibull finite endpoint for bounded accuracy) is plausible but I found no recent application; random restarts are not iid draws from one tail, so it needs checking (my inference).
- Attacker scaling laws. Jailbreaks: power law in samples (#14), adaptivity beats static (#15), family matters (#16). Membership: aggregation matters more than raw compute (Puerto), strong attacks plateau at modest AUC on LLMs (Hayes). Representation privacy (identity from masks/latents): I found no scaling-law study. Treat this as open, and possibly because it is boring (plateau at the first retrained attacker).
- Sample complexity of attackers. Only indirect evidence: Wheat (BER needs), Picture the Epsilon (bound is zero at n<=10 per class, positive at ~30 per class only for very large epsilon), learning-curve literature [S].
- Worst case over attackers with honest CI, and the max's bias. Tools exist but are not applied to privacy suites: BI-style union-bound certificate (#8), dev/test split with fixed rule (#7), winner's-curse corrections (#18, #19, QJE 2024 [S]), frozen shortlist (#19). My back-of-envelope (my arithmetic, equal true means, independent errors, so an upper bound on the bias): expected maximum of K standard normals is 0.56, 1.16, 1.54, 1.87 sigma for K=2, 5, 10, 20. With the KTH test of 598 clips and a clip-level binomial s.e. of ~1.3 points at p=0.11, K=10 gives ~2 points; if the effective unit count is ~10x smaller because clips within a recording are dependent, ~6 points. Programme 1's margins at the low-leakage end are 2-3 points (e.g. x16 canonical pose 13.7 vs learned x64 11.4), so selection and unit dependence are the same size as the effects. Attackers evaluated on the same test units share noise, so the true bias is smaller; it must be measured, not assumed.

### Recipe for reporting a worst case honestly (assembled from #7, #8, #18, #19, #20; not a published standard)
1. Declare the suite (families, hyperparameter grid, K) before any number; hash it.
2. Split by independent unit (recording/person for HAR, scan/patient for CT): attacker-train, attacker-val, sealed test. Select hyperparameters and the "worst" member on val only.
3. Report three numbers: naive max over members on test (for comparison only), val-selected member on the sealed test with a cluster bootstrap CI, and the suite upper bound max_k UCB_k with Bonferroni over K (BI style, effective n from clusters). State that the last is suite-conditional.
4. Report the tightness: the smallest effect the sealed test can resolve (MDE80 from effective n, as in programme 1's power audits) and the epsilon at which the bound becomes non-zero.
5. If the suite is enlarged adaptively on the same test units, switch to conditional or locally simultaneous intervals or a frozen shortlist (#18, #19).
6. Always keep one attacker family out of the selection and out of any defender training, and report it separately (see D2).

### D1. Sealed, cluster-aware suite certificate ("BI for HAR and CT")
- What: a deterministic scorer layer that takes saved per-unit predictions from a pre-declared suite (LDA, kNN, LightGBM, MLP, a video/3D network, a foundation-feature probe) and emits the recipe above as a JSON report: naive max, val-selected sealed-test value with cluster bootstrap CI, Bonferroni/KL-binomial suite upper bound, effective n, MDE80, and the zero-bound threshold. Extends the existing README scorers (hash splits, byte-identical output).
- Deterministic verification: closed form on prediction files; hashes of suite and predictions are part of the report. Nothing stochastic after attackers are trained; training itself is covered by the sealed-test and seed rules already in the charter.
- Compute: closed-form; rerunning attackers once to save predictions is hours on KTH.
- Biggest risk: honest intervals will be wide. With roughly 100 recordings as independent units, a 95% Clopper-Pearson half-width near 10% accuracy is ~6 points before the union bound; Bonferroni over K=30 raises the one-sided z from 1.64 to ~2.94 (roughly +80% width; my arithmetic). Most small programme-1 differences become "not resolved". It remains suite-conditional (the BI authors' own limitation), so it is a reporting standard, not a guarantee.
- Trivial floor that kills it: the effective-unit count. If MDE80 from clusters exceeds the margin of interest (about 5 points) on KTH and CT, the certificate collapses to "chance plus width" for every defender worth discussing. Gate: compute design effect and MDE80 from existing splits before building anything (an hour, no GPU).
- Novelty note: BI (#8) is side-channel; Picture the Epsilon (#7) is face embeddings; I found nothing for HAR or CT.

### D2. Attacker ladder, saturation rule and leave-one-family-out (LOFO) coverage
- What: for a fixed panel of ~20 released representations (raw, blur, down16/32/64, fixed motion maps, BDQ pools, canonical pose, plus CT latents), grow the attacker along four axes: training-data fraction (1/16 to 1), capacity (linear to MLP to deep to pretrained backbone), optimisation budget (steps, restarts), family. Report the envelope L*(c) with cluster-bootstrap CIs. Pre-registered stop rule: stop adding rungs when the envelope gains less than delta (e.g. 2 points, upper CI below delta) over two consecutive doublings in at least 90% of panel cells. Validate any extrapolation by fitting only the lower rungs and predicting the top two (the BoN-style check); no extrapolation is reported if that fails. LOFO: recompute the suite worst case with each family removed; the "coverage" is how much the worst case drops. This is the measurement half of the charter's falsifiable core (does a defender trained on a suite hold up on a family it never saw).
- Deterministic verification: fixed seeds and splits; rungs defined by hashed configs; envelope and stop decision computed by a script from saved predictions. GPU non-determinism affects individual fits, so the decision uses seed-replicated CIs.
- Compute: ~20 cells x ~25 ladder points = ~500 attacker fits; linear/kNN/GBM rungs are CPU; deep rungs ~10 min each on KTH latents gives ~40-80 GPU-h, one day on 4 GPUs. CT with precomputed latents is similar; end-to-end 3D attackers are the expensive part.
- Biggest risk: compression at both ends. Programme 1's table has many cells near 95-99% (raw, blur, down16) or near chance (x64, 1 px); only the middle band (about x24 to x48, BDQ, pose without the last redraw) can show scaling, and bounded, noisy curves near a floor make power-law-plus-offset fits unreliable. Larger attackers may also overfit at ~24 clips per person.
- Trivial floor that kills it: a retrained LDA or kNN attacker already matches the ladder envelope within CI on at least 90% of cells (programme 1's C found LDA the strongest identity attacker on CREMA-D; A found LDA-type floors hard to beat). Then the result is "a suite of {LDA, kNN, LightGBM, one deep net} saturates; scaling is flat", which is a one-day negative result with a minimal-suite recommendation, not a programme.
- Novelty: I found no study of attacker scaling or held-out attacker families for representation privacy; adjacent evidence only (#14-#17).

### D3. Selection-bias budget of the attacker maximum (cheap gate for D1)
- What: using saved predictions, (i) simulate selection of the max among K attackers on test vs on val, with real inter-attacker correlation; (ii) compare naive max-of-test with the val-selected sealed value across programme-1 cells; (iii) check coverage of cluster-bootstrap, Bonferroni, and locally-simultaneous intervals (#18) on a synthetic identity generator whose true Bayes accuracy is known, so the true maximum is known.
- Deterministic verification: seeds fixed; coverage of each interval is an explicit pass/fail.
- Compute: hours, mostly CPU.
- Biggest risk: with K=2-3 per run (programme 1) and many classes (#20) the inflation is probably smaller than training-seed noise, i.e. the answer is "negligible", which is fine as a gate but not a contribution.
- Trivial floor: inflation below 1 point in every programme-1 cell and K up to 10: drop selection corrections, keep only the sealed split. It matters if the programme grows K to 20-30.

### D4. Calibrated log-loss (V-information) as the headline statistic
- What: report I_V = H(ID) - cross-entropy of temperature-scaled retrained attackers, in bits, with cluster-bootstrap CIs, alongside top-1; build the V-ladder (nested families) for the monotone "information the attacker can use" curve. Hypothesis: lower variance and smaller MDE than top-1 exactly in the low-leakage band where defenders operate.
- Deterministic verification: closed form on saved probabilities; calibration set fixed.
- Compute: rerun attackers saving probabilities; hours to a day.
- Biggest risk: overconfident deep attackers make log-loss dominated by a few confident errors; the estimate can be negative (seen in Makonnen et al.); identity saturates at ln C and top-1 may track the re-identification threat better than average code length.
- Trivial floor: if the MDE80 of log-loss and top-1 on the same predictions differ by less than ~10%, or the ranking of the 20 cells is identical, there is no gain.

### D5. Certified ceiling for noise-dithered releases
- What: for Z = clip(g(x)) + N(0, sigma^2 I), the Gaussian-channel bound I(Z;X) <= 0.5 * sum log(1 + lambda_i/sigma^2) (standard, from the latent covariance) plus a Fano-type bound (#13) gives an attacker-independent ceiling on identity accuracy. Report ceiling next to the measured suite worst case; the gap is the looseness. This gives the programme-1 finding "dither matches the adversarial AE at equal leakage" a provable side that no learned unrandomised encoder has; a learned encoder plus calibrated noise plus a capacity penalty is the learned counterpart.
- Deterministic verification: closed form given covariance and sigma.
- Compute: hours.
- Biggest risk: vacuous. With 25 persons ln 25 = 3.2 nats, Fano only bites when I is below about one nat, which probably means sigma large enough to destroy action accuracy; identity-level classifier bounds (#7) are zero at these sample sizes.
- Trivial floor that kills it: certified ceiling above 0.5 at every sigma that keeps at least 90% of action accuracy.

### D6. One-run canary audit of a released CT encoder (different threat model)
- What: insert randomised canary patients into encoder training on LUNA25 and audit membership of the released weights with the one-run f-DP method (#9, #10) or the zero-run correction (#11). Learned and novel for non-DP medical encoders.
- Biggest risk: wrong threat model for this programme (membership of training patients, not re-identification of released latents); valid audit bounds are tied to DP-style randomisation; tightness limited by canary interference (Keinan et al.); needs a training run, so not byte-deterministic.
- Trivial floor: a loss-threshold membership attack already saturates at AUC ~0.5 on a frozen foundation encoder, in which case there is nothing to audit.
- Recommendation: park unless the CT side explicitly includes weight release.

## 5. Not recommended as deliverables (cheap gates only)
- kNN / GHP / soft-label Bayes-error estimators and MI estimators (MINE, InfoNCE, CLUB). Reasons: sample needs (#6) exceed HAR data by an order of magnitude; estimators are asymptotic and are themselves attackers (a kNN attacker is already in the suite, so a saturating kNN Bayes estimate adds nothing); MI estimators fail in high dimension and cannot certify large leaks (#2, #3). If used at all, validate with FeeBee-style injected identity noise on a HAR embedding first and drop them if they do not track the injected noise.
- PAC-privacy (instance-level, simulation-based) and statistic maximal leakage (deterministic quantisers): interesting for certified releases but about the release mechanism, not about measuring an arbitrary learned mask.

## 6. Overall read and suggested order
Order: D3 (hours, no GPU) and the D1 power gate first; then D1 as the instrument; then D2 as the experiment that bears on the charter's question. D4 is a statistic to try inside D1. D5 is a side project to attach a provable ceiling to the dither floor. D6 parked.
Main sceptical caveats: (a) programme 1's signal may already be saturated by the first retrained attacker, so the suite may collapse to a few members; (b) the effective sample size (recordings, persons, patients) is the binding constraint on any "honest CI", more than the choice of estimator; (c) a certificate is only as good as the declared suite, and BI-style local neighbourhoods (BI^loc) are untested outside side-channel work; (d) defenders optimised against a fixed suite will beat it (#12), so a sealed held-out family is part of the measurement, not an extra.
