# Scout: robust adversary suites and populations (programme 2, 2026-11-04)

Cluster: standardised attack suites, adaptive attacks, minimax against attacker populations, how many/which attackers, transfer to unseen attacker families.
Window: 2024-2026 first; older papers only where a recent one points back (marked "older").
Reading depth is marked per paper: FULL = opened the arXiv HTML and read at least the first ~100k characters; ABS = opened the arXiv abstract page only;
SNIP = seen only in a search-result snippet (not opened; treat as unverified). Nothing below is cited from memory except where a line says "memory, unverified".
Read first: CHARTER.md, privacy-obfuscation/results/SUMMARY.md, fields/FOCUS.md, the bottom of fields/journal/2026-11-04.md, and the CT-experiment README / ct-status (worktree `remote-ct-branch-explore-b1010f`, not on this branch).

## 0. What our own results already say (so the scoring below is not naive)

- HAR video (KTH, Charades, VP-UCF101/HMDB51, UTD-MHAD): frozen raw-trained attackers understate identity leakage by a median 47 points (KTH), 57.9 (Charades), 81.7 (UTD-MHAD); retrained attackers recover 95-99% KTH identity through blur/pixelation/x16 downsampling. DINOv2 attackers beat the standard (ResNet-50 + R3D-18) suite on VP-HMDB51 (certified privacy 43.4 vs 50-59 for the standard re-runs), which is why the harness has a "certified = worst over re-runs" rule. A suite already exists informally; nothing has measured how many members it needs.
- CT (CT-RATE, frozen CT-FM, cross-visit linkage): best-of-5 retrained attackers; adapters A3/A21 cut linkage 0.474 -> 0.16-0.19 at ~full utility but stay 3.7-4.5x above the scanner-metadata floor (0.042); adapters beat plain noise only at utility >= ~0.85. No pixel-space attacker was run.
- Programme 1 negatives that constrain every direction below: label-only LDA, per-source whitening, rate bottleneck and noise dither match learned adapters at equal leakage; LDA is the strongest identity attacker on CREMA-D; closed-form erasure fitted on seen units fails on unseen units.

## 2. What is known, per sub-question

(a) Standardised, parameter-free suites. AutoAttack (1) works because the perturbation set is bounded, so "the best attack" is a well-posed optimisation. No privacy analogue exists as a fixed-code, defence-agnostic package: searches for "AutoAttack for privacy" returned only the original AutoAttack and the MIA ensemble (4). The nearest things are (i) the VoicePrivacy attacker tiers and challenge (7), where the standard is a protocol (knowledge level, training data, EER) plus a crowd; (ii) MIBench for model inversion; (iii) MIA ensembles. For attribute/identity inference on released latents or masked video/signals, no suite or leaderboard was found. To be parameter-free a privacy suite must fix what AutoAttack gets from the Lp ball: attacker knowledge, training data budget, capacity, and epochs.

(b) Adaptive attacks and why defences fail. The 2024-26 privacy examples all say the same: static or non-adaptive evaluation overestimates privacy (3, 7, 8 and the TRACE-RPS appendix). Failure causes are consistent: average-case instead of worst-unit metrics, fixed attacker not retrained on the defended output (our Finding 1), attack not tailored to the defence mechanism, and no tuned trivial baseline (3). Only under 10% of privacy-preserving action-recognition papers use adaptive attackers (8, abstract-level).

(c) Minimax against populations. In RL, a uniform adversary population already beats a single adversary (12); PSRO-style meta-solvers are described in surveys (IJCAI 2024 PSRO survey, SNIP; arXiv 2403.00420, SNIP), but I found no application of PSRO/double-oracle to privacy representations or HAR/CT. In privacy, the multi-adversary ideas are old (14, 15) and recent representation-privacy papers use one adversary (17). Rainbow Teaming (13) is the quality-diversity route. Explicit attacker-diversity regularisation for privacy defenders: not found.

(d) How many/which attackers. Evidence is scattered: AutoAttack needs four; the VPAC winner was best on 6 of 7 systems (7), i.e. a dominant "fine-tune a strong pretrained backbone on the anonymised data" attacker plus a minority specialist; MIA instances of one attack disagree as much as attacks do (4); in HAR sensors tree models and CNNs disagree by 2-4x (20); in our own VP-HMDB51 run DINOv2 beat the standard pair. No paper gives a saturation or coverage curve for privacy attackers: that is a gap.

(e) Transfer to unseen families. Adversarial-robustness side: robustness does not transfer by default (9, 10) and populations help (12). Privacy side: in-training adversaries at chance do not predict a fresh attacker (16); I found no 2024-26 systematic leave-one-attacker-family-out study for privacy defences (17 and the latent-anonymiser papers in "also seen" evaluate unseen tasks, not unseen attackers). Gap.

### A. Fixed-code attacker suite with coverage curves (recommended)
- What: freeze 8-12 attackers spanning families (LDA/logistic, kNN with cosine AND Canberra, LightGBM/RF, MLP, sequence/transformer head, frozen-FM probe, metric-learning verification head as in 19/Packhauser, end-to-end R3D/ResNet, LoRA-fine-tuned foundation model as in 7), each with a fixed data/epoch/seed budget and 3+ restarts (4: restarts matter). Score every programme-1 defence (KTH stick-figure/BDQ/fixed motion maps, Charades, VP-HMDB51, MotionSense, CT-RATE A3/A21/noise/PCA) by worst-over-suite with per-member, union and leave-one-member-out curves, plus a per-unit worst-case (per-person TAR at fixed FAR, bottom decile of persons) alongside mean top-1 (3).
- Falsifiable claim (matches the charter): the suite changes a programme-1 conclusion, i.e. flips a defence's rank or crosses a pre-registered "private" threshold, or does not.
- Data: HAR video KTH (25 people), Charades (90 homes), VP-UCF101/HMDB51 attribute labels: all already in the pipeline (licences as handled in programme 1). Wearables: MotionSense (24 subjects, MIT, verified), PAMAP2 (9 subjects, CC BY 4.0, verified; too few identities for identity claims). Capture-24 (151 participants, 100 Hz wrist; free via Oxford ORA; licence and the "do not attempt to identify individuals" term unverified, see section 5). CT: CT-RATE (CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, gated, no redistribution, 25,692 volumes / 21,304 patients, verified) with existing frozen CT-FM latents.
- Compute: frozen-feature attackers are seconds to minutes; only the heavy members (R3D-18, ResNet-50, fine-tuned DINOv2) cost 0.3-1 GPU-h. Roughly 100-200 GPU-h for the heavy members across defences x 3 seeds; 2-3 days on 4 GPUs if the video part is restricted to KTH + one more set.
- Biggest risk: collapse to one dominant attacker (VPAC: one backbone-fine-tune won 6 of 7; programme 1: LDA strongest on CREMA-D), so the "suite" adds nothing over one strong attacker plus budget; and "parameter-free" is by fiat because the attacker class is unbounded.
- Cheap trivial floor that would kill it: the envelope of three cheap attackers (LDA, kNN-Canberra, linear probe on frozen DINOv2/CT-FM features) plus the metadata-only floor. If suite-worst minus cheap-3 envelope is within the MDE in at least ~90% of defence cells, the answer is "three cheap attackers are the suite" and the programme reports that (a useful negative, and the suite shrinks to those three). Pre-register the MDE from the real number of independent people/patients first (25 people on KTH; 8 on UTD-MHAD is unusable).

### C. Held-out attacker-family generalisation (recommended; highest risk)
- What: train defenders against a population from families {F1..Fk}, evaluate worst-case leakage against a held-out family Fk+1 (leave-one-family-out over 5-6 families), versus defenders trained against a single strongest attacker with restarts (12), versus the floors. Start on latent adapters (CT-FM latents, cached MotionSense/PAMAP2 encoder features) where one defender trains in minutes; confirm on KTH BDQ only if the latent result is positive.
- Why: the second falsifiable core of the charter; the literature gap is real (section 2e). Evidence for failure modes: in-training adversary at chance but fresh attacker above chance (16); MARS-style subspace ensembles exist (15) but were never tested on held-out families as far as the snippets show.
- Compute: latent-level, hours per full leave-one-out grid at 3 seeds. Video confirmation: ~29 runs' worth, a day or two.
- Biggest risk: programme 1 already found learned adversarial methods tie rate bottleneck/noise dither at equal leakage, so the population may add nothing; also "family" is a soft notion (is MLP vs kNN a different family or only a different inductive bias?), so the held-out test must use structurally different members (e.g., tree model and a generative/inversion attacker held out together).
- Cheap floors that kill it: (i) noise-dithered or rate-bottlenecked code with no adversary at matched utility and leakage (programme 1 saw a dithered AE match the adversarial AE); (ii) single-attacker-with-restarts defender generalising equally; (iii) label-only LDA projection. Kill if the population-trained defender's held-out-family worst-case leakage is not below the lower envelope of all floors by the pre-registered margin.

### B. Attacker knowledge x capacity ladder
- What: define rungs L0 frozen raw-trained attacker; L1 retrained on released outputs (programme 1); L2 defence-aware (knows the mechanism; trains on mixtures of defence strengths and, where a paired raw/obfuscated set is a justified threat, a learned de-obfuscator in front of L1); L3 foundation-model fine-tune with a larger data budget (the VPAC winner pattern, 7). Report the rung-to-rung gap for each defence; this is the "knowledge" axis of A.
- Data: same as A. Compute: moderate; L3 adds fine-tuning cost (~1 GPU-h per cell).
- Risk: threat-model creep. Anyone with paired data inverts any invertible transform, so the likely finding is "deterministic canonical forms fall at L2", which may be true but trivial. Floor that kills it: rate-bottleneck/noise-dither (non-invertible by construction) at matched utility: if L2/L3 leak equally on floors and learned defences, the ladder says nothing specific to learned methods. State the paired-data threat model up front or drop L2's de-obfuscator.

### E. Quality-diversity / agent search over attackers
- What: an autoresearch loop that proposes attacker configurations (architecture, features, augmentation, loss, data subset) and keeps an archive indexed by descriptors (family, capacity, data access); the archive is frozen into deterministic code and then becomes the suite for A. Fits the user's "autoresearch loop" request, and the VPAC crowd is replaced by an agent population.
- Compute: each trial is a few minutes on cached features; 500 trials is under a day on 4 GPUs.
- Risk: search finds a better hyperparameter setting of a standard attacker, not a new family; adaptive overfitting to the development defences; agent non-determinism (the final frozen suite is deterministic, the search is not). Floor that kills it: random search over the same template grid with the same trial budget, and tuned LDA/kNN. Evaluate found attackers on sealed defences never shown to the search.

### D. PSRO / double-oracle with an exploitability stop rule
- What: alternate defender best response and attacker best response; keep the attacker pool; measure population exploitability and stop when a new best-response attacker no longer raises leakage. This turns "how many attackers" into a convergence criterion.
- Risk: privacy-utility is not a clean zero-sum game (needs a Lagrangian or a utility constraint); best-response oracles are approximate and overfit finite samples; uniform sampling of random inits already worked in RL (12). Floor that kills it: uniform pool of K random-restart attackers (RAP) and single attacker with checkpoint replay at the same compute. Worth doing only as an arm inside C.

### F. Open longitudinal CT linkage benchmark (NLST)
- What: CT-RATE is gated, non-redistributable and its terms prohibit re-identification, which blocks a releasable, fully deterministic CT benchmark. NLST on TCIA is CC BY 4.0 with 26,254 subjects and 73,116 CT studies (about 2.8 per subject, three annual screens), 11.3 TB in total (verified). A fixed 2,000-subject subset (~0.9 TB, my estimate from 155 MB per study) with frozen split lists, CT-FM / other encoder latents, and verification-style metrics (TAR at fixed FAR, bottom-decile patients) would make the CT side of A and C releasable. LIDC-IDRI (CC BY 3.0, 1,010 patients, but only 8 with two timepoints) is for cross-dataset checks, not linkage.
- Risk: scanner/site/protocol shortcut. Patients are rescanned at the same screening site, so linkage may be mostly DICOM-header and field-of-view identity; the CT work already needed a scanner-metadata floor (0.042 on CT-RATE). TCIA's policy forbids using data "to identify or contact individual participants" (verified); within-dataset pseudonymous linkage is arguably different but should be confirmed with the owner. Floor that kills it: header-only + body-size/FOV linkage within a few points of raw-latent linkage.

## 4. Directions I would NOT pursue

1. A new multi-discriminator anonymiser for raw video or CT pixels as a method paper: MARS (15) and Wu et al. (14) are prior art, programme 1 found learned ties cheap floors, and video training is the most expensive thing on the machine. Population training enters only as an arm of C on latents.
2. Certified or information-theoretic guarantees (CLUB/MINE bounds as in 17, DP on latents): bounds are stated against one trained adversary and binary attributes; a guarantee-versus-measurement programme belongs to the leakage scout, and a certificate would not say anything about unseen retrained attackers, our actual question.
3. A crowdsourced attacker challenge in the VPAC style: 41 registered, 11 submitted; needs a community and months. The cheap version is E.
4. LLM jailbreak / prompt-injection red teaming as a domain: Nasr, Zhan, Andriushchenko, Self-RedTeam teach method (adaptive RL/search, restarts, self-play) but the data, verifiers and judge noise do not transfer; do not build there.
5. Adversarial-example robustness of skeleton recognisers (TASAR, RobustBenchHAR): a different meaning of "adversarial" (input perturbation), no privacy content. Only the benchmark layout is worth borrowing.
6. NTU RGB+D as a main dataset: registration plus a release agreement, academic use only, and the page lists creating derived datasets and redistribution as not permitted without permission (verified), so nothing built on it can be released. At most a private confirmation.
7. Tiny-identity HAR sets for identity claims (UTD-MHAD 8 people, PAMAP2 9, HHAR 9): our own results show everyone is identifiable even from stick figures (UTD-MHAD) and the number of independent units makes any margin unpowered.
8. "Stronger attacker always wins" scaling without a stop rule: with no bounded perturbation set the program never terminates; every scaling arm needs the saturation criterion of D or A first.
9. Pixel-space CT reconstruction / diffusion inversion as a first move: owed from the CT work and relevant, but expensive and the foundation-model/generative-attacker scout owns it; add it as a held-out family in C only after the latent results.

## 6. Recommendation for the programme

Build A and B as one suite with a knowledge axis, make C its headline generalisation test, and use E only to populate and extend the frozen suite. Pre-register before any suite number: the cheap-3 attacker envelope and the metadata-only floor as the kill floors for A; the leave-one-family-out margin against the lower envelope of rate-bottleneck, dither and LDA for C; an MDE from the real count of independent people/patients; a frozen sealed set of defences that E never sees. Treat D as an arm inside C, F as a data decision to put to the owner (licence and re-identification clause), and everything in section 4 as parked.

Open items not resolved here: PSRO or QD applied to privacy representations (no paper found, so novelty is a search-limited claim); the VoicePrivacy per-system EER table (Fig. 2 of 2504.14183 was not readable); the full text of the PPAR survey's evaluation protocol; whether Capture-24 and TCIA terms permit within-dataset identity linkage.
